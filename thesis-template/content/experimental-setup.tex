\chapter{Experimental setup}
\label{chap:experimental-setup}

This chapter describes how the contributions were evaluated: with which parameters the cell was simulated
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-parameters}), where the implementation departs from the original scheme
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-differences}), which methods were compared (Section~\ref{sec:exp-methods}), which runs
were made (Section~\ref{sec:exp-runs}), by which measures each method was judged
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-metrics}), and how the methods were compared with each other
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-comparisons}).

\label{sec:exp-differences}

gathered in Table~\ref{tab:exp-differences}. Some are simplifications of the scenario. Others are corrections,

\section{Compared methods}
% Las dos reglas fijas (Greedy y la regla de umbral) y los tres métodos que aprenden (DTE, FedAvg y gossip).
\label{sec:exp-methods}

The comparison includes two fixed rules, which do not learn, and three learning methods, which differ only
in how the agents share what they learn (Table~\ref{tab:exp-methods}).

\paragraph{Greedy.} This is the greedy rule with which \citet{li+22fedmarl} compare their scheme: each pair
chooses the channel with the least interference from other pairs and always transmits at maximum power. Here,
the interference is estimated with the only information that an agent has about the other pairs: each pair
chooses the channel that the fewest neighbors used in the previous slot, $\textstyle\argmin_m N_k^{t-1}[m]$, and breaks
ties at random. Breaking them at random matters. If ties go to the channel with the lowest index, all the
pairs see the same counts in the first slot, choose the same channel, and then jump together from channel to
channel, which makes the rule artificially weak.

\paragraph{Threshold rule.} This rule is not in \citet{li+22fedmarl}; we designed it as a reference for what
can be achieved with local information and knowledge of the problem, without learning. It chooses the channel
as Greedy does, but it decides whether to transmit from two values of its own observation:
\begin{equation}
  P_k^t =
  \begin{cases}
    \Pmax, & \text{if } \bar h_k^t \ge \SI{-91}{dB} \text{ or } Q_k^t \ge 0.9\, \Qmax , \\
    0, & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:exp-threshold}
\end{equation}
where $\bar h_k^t$ is the mean of the observed gains of its own link over the $M$ channels, in dB. Since the
fast fading has mean 1, this mean estimates the large-scale gain of the link, that is, how far away the
receiver is; \SI{-91}{dB} is the gain of a \SI{25}{\meter} link. A pair whose receiver is close always
transmits. A pair whose receiver is far stays silent, which protects the \ac{CUE} of its channel, until its
queue reaches 90\% of the maximum; then it transmits. The rule embodies an insight about the problem (weak
links contribute little and harm a lot), not a general procedure. For that reason, it is not a proposal of
this thesis but a yardstick.

\paragraph{DTE.} Each pair has its own \ac{D3QN} agent and learns alone, sharing nothing with anyone
(decentralized training and execution, Section~\ref{sec:bg-marl}). It is the control: it shows how much
sharing contributes.

\paragraph{FedAvg.} The agents average their networks at the \ac{BS} every 50 slots
(Section~\ref{sec:pa-fedavg}). With the shared reward, it is the original scheme of \citet{li+22fedmarl}.

\paragraph{Gossip.} The agents average their networks with their neighbors within \SI{300}{\meter} every 50
slots (Section~\ref{sec:pa-gossip}). With the individual reward, it is the proposal of this thesis. The
variants with $S = 2$ and $S = 3$ cap the number of neighbors of each round (Section~\ref{sec:pa-gossip-s}).

\section{Runs}
% Qué es una corrida (un método, un K y una semilla durante 2000 episodios) y los cuatro barridos: recompensa individual, recompensa compartida y gossip con S = 2 y con S = 3.
\label{sec:exp-runs}

A \termdef{run} trains one method with one value of $K$ and one seed for 2000 episodes. The runs are organized
in four \termdef{sweeps}, and each sweep covers the six values of $K$ with seeds 0, 1, and 2:
\begin{description}
  \item[Individual reward.] \ac{DTE}, \ac{FedAvg}, and gossip, learning from the individual reward
    (Equation~\ref{eq:pa-individual-reward}), plus Greedy and the threshold rule: 90 runs.
  \item[Shared reward.] The same five methods, with the agents learning from the shared reward
    (Equation~\ref{eq:sm-shared-reward}): 90 runs. Greedy and the threshold rule do not learn and, with the
    same seed, see the same scenarios, so they give exactly the same results in both sweeps.
  \item[Gossip with $S = 2$ and with $S = 3$.] Only the corresponding variant, with the individual reward:
    18 runs each.
\end{description}
In total, there are 216 runs. The first two sweeps differ only in the reward from which the agents learn;
everything else, including $\gamma = 0.9$, is the same. The difference between them therefore measures the
effect of the reward, and the difference between gossip and \ac{FedAvg} within one sweep measures the effect
of the aggregation.

\paragraph{Same seed, same scenarios.} Episode $e$ of a run with seed $\varsigma$ draws its positions and its
fading from the seed $10^6 \varsigma + e$. Since the number of random draws in each slot does not depend on the
actions, two runs with the same $K$ and the same seed see exactly the same positions and the same channels in
every episode, whatever the method. The agents and the random choice of neighbors of the capped variant use
streams of their own, derived from the same seed, so they do not disturb those draws.

\paragraph{Preliminary diagnosis.} Before the sweeps, a diagnosis looked for the reason why our implementation
of the original scheme lost performance toward the end of training, and it fixed the configuration of the
sweeps. It consists of 24 runs, all with \ac{FedAvg}, $K = 10$, seed 0, and 2000 episodes, each changing one
or two options at a time. Appendix~\ref{apx:diagnosis} presents it.

\paragraph{Implementation and execution.} The simulator and the agents are written in Python, with PyTorch
\cite{paszke+19pytorch} for the networks. The environment follows the parallel interface of PettingZoo
\cite{terry+21pettingzoo}, designed for agents that act at the same time. It computes the shared reward in
every slot, and it hands each agent, separately, the reward from which it learns, so that every run is
logged with the same measure. 191 automated tests check the implementation. Some compare each equation of the
model with values computed by hand; others check properties that must always hold, for example that gossip
on a graph in which everyone is a neighbor of everyone gives the same result as \ac{FedAvg}, that an isolated
agent keeps its model, and that no aggregation touches the target networks. The sweeps ran on processors of
Hypatia, the high-performance computing cluster of Universidad de los Andes, for a total of about 800 hours
of computation. The tables and figures of Chapter~\ref{chap:results} are generated by a script directly from
the logs of the runs. Appendix~\ref{apx:reproducibility} explains how to repeat the experiments.

\section{Metrics}
% Recompensa por episodio (medida principal), valor final y medidas de calidad de servicio, comportamiento, comunicación, consenso y aprendizaje.
\label{sec:exp-metrics}

\paragraph{Reward per episode.} The main measure is the shared reward (Equation~\ref{eq:sm-shared-reward})
summed over the 200 slots of an episode. It sums up in a single number the three objectives of the problem:
the rate of the \acp{CUE}, and the delay and the reliability of the pairs. Every method is measured with it,
including those that learn from the individual reward and those that do not learn.

\paragraph{Final value.} The final value of a measure in a run is its mean over the last 10\% of the episodes,
episodes 1801 to 2000. In those episodes, exploration is already at its floor of 0.05, so the final value
reflects what the method learned. For each method and each $K$, the tables give the mean and the standard
deviation of the final values of the three seeds.

\paragraph{Quality of service.} Three measures, averaged over the slots and the pairs:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item the \termdef{rate of the \acp{CUE}}: the mean rate of the 15 \acp{CUE}, in Mbps;
  \item the \termdef{outage}: the percentage of slots in which a pair fails the reliability condition
    ($\mu_k^t < 0$). A silent pair counts as in outage (Equation~\ref{eq:sm-margin}), so this measure mixes
    two things: links that transmit without enough margin, and links that do not transmit;
  \item the \termdef{delay}: the queue at the start of the slot divided by $\lambda$ (Little's law), in
    milliseconds. It exceeds $\Dmax$ exactly when the queue exceeds $\Qmax$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Behavior.} Three measures describe how the pairs use the spectrum: the \termdef{channels in use}
(how many different channels the transmitting pairs occupy, on average), the \termdef{mean power} (as a
fraction of $\Pmax$, counting silent slots as zero), and the percentage of slots in which a pair transmits on a
channel on which another pair also transmits.

\paragraph{Communication.} Per episode, the number of model transfers, the megabytes they move, and the load
of the busiest node (Section~\ref{sec:pa-comm-cost}).

\paragraph{Consensus.} The \termdef{weight dispersion}: the mean Euclidean distance between the weights of
the online network of each agent and the average of the weights of all the agents. It is measured right
before each round, at the same instants also for \ac{DTE}, and averaged over the episode. It is zero when all
the agents have the same network, and it grows as their networks drift apart.

\paragraph{Learning.} Three measures describe the reward curve of each run:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item the \termdef{improvement}: the final value minus the mean of the first 50 episodes;
  \item the \termdef{speed}: the first episode at which the moving average over 50 episodes covers 90\% of
    the improvement. This measure is used instead of a classic criterion of convergence because the curves
    keep moving until the end (Section~\ref{sec:res-learning});
  \item the \termdef{forgetting}: the best mean over a block of 200 consecutive episodes (1--200, 201--400,
    and so on) minus the mean of the last block. It measures how much of what the method achieved is lost at
    the end.
\end{itemize}

\section{How the methods are compared}
% Comparaciones pareadas semilla por semilla, prueba de signos y lo que no se compara.
\label{sec:exp-comparisons}

\paragraph{Paired comparisons.} Since two runs with the same $K$ and the same seed see the same scenarios
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-runs}), the methods are compared seed by seed. Besides the difference between the means,
the tables give in how many of the three seeds one method beats the other. This separates the effect of the
method from the luck of the geometry: a method that wins in all three seeds wins in three different sets of
scenarios.

\paragraph{Sign test.} To judge whether a count of wins could be due to chance, the sign test is used. If two
methods were equally good, each paired comparison would be won by either of them with probability $1/2$, and
the probability that one of them wins at least $x$ of $n$ independent comparisons would be
\begin{equation}
  \Pr(X \ge x) = \sum_{i = x}^{n} \binom{n}{i} \frac{1}{2^{n}}
  \label{eq:exp-sign-test}
\end{equation}
where $X$ is the number of wins. For example, winning all 18 comparisons of six values of $K$ with three seeds
has a probability of $2^{-18} \approx 4 \times 10^{-6}$ if the methods are equally good. The test assumes
that the comparisons are independent, which here holds only approximately: runs with the same seed and
different $K$ share the seed, although their scenarios differ. For this reason, these probabilities are read
as indications of how unlikely a result is, not as formal proofs.

\paragraph{What is not compared.} The learning curves are shown as the mean of the three seeds, smoothed with
a moving average of 50 episodes. They show the dynamics, but the conclusions rest on the final values and on
the paired comparisons. The results of the diagnosis, which use a single seed, serve only to choose the
configuration, not to compare methods.
