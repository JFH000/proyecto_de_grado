\chapter{System model and problem formulation}
\label{chap:system-model}

This chapter describes the simulated world in which the agents learn: where the vehicles and the cellular
users are, how the signals propagate, how the queues fill and empty, and what it means for a link to be
reliable. With these elements, it states the resource allocation problem and turns it into a multi-agent
learning problem, with observations, actions, and a reward. The model follows that of
\citet{li+22fedmarl}. Where this thesis simplifies or corrects it, the text says so, and
Section~\ref{sec:exp-differences} gathers all the differences.

\section{Scenario}
% Celda de 500 m de radio con la BS en el centro, 15 CUE con un canal de subida cada uno, K pares V2V (5 a 30), cómo se sortean las posiciones y quiénes son vecinos (300 m o menos).
\label{sec:sm-scenario}

The scenario is a circular cell with a radius of \SI{500}{\meter} and a \ac{BS} at its center
(Figure~\ref{fig:sm-scenario}). The cell contains $M = 15$ \acp{CUE}, each with its own uplink channel of
\SI{1}{\mega\hertz}: \ac{CUE} $m$ transmits to the \ac{BS} on channel $m$, with
$m \in \Mset = \{1, \dots, M\}$. It also contains $K$ \ac{V2V} pairs, indexed by
$k \in \Kset = \{1, \dots, K\}$; in the experiments, $K$ takes the values 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30. The
pairs have no channels of their own: each one must reuse the channel of some \ac{CUE}.

Time advances in intervals of $\tau = \SI{1}{\milli\second}$, called \termdef{slots}. In each slot $t$, each
pair $k$ chooses a channel $m_k^t \in \Mset$ and a transmit power $P_k^t$ among $N_p + 1 = 4$ levels:
\begin{equation}
  P_k^t \in \mathcal{P} = \Bigl\{ 0,\; \tfrac{1}{3}\Pmax,\; \tfrac{2}{3}\Pmax,\; \Pmax \Bigr\}
  \label{eq:sm-power-levels}
\end{equation}
where $\Pmax = \SI{23}{dBm}$, about \SI{200}{\milli\watt}, is the maximum power. Choosing zero power means
staying silent in that slot. The indicator $\rho_k^t[m]$ is 1 if pair $k$ chose channel $m$ in slot $t$, and
0 otherwise. The \acp{CUE} always transmit, with a fixed power $P^{\mathrm{c}} = \SI{23}{dBm}$.

The positions are drawn at the start of each \termdef{episode}, a block of 200 slots (\SI{0.2}{\second}).
The \acp{CUE} and the \ac{V2V} transmitters are placed uniformly at random in the cell, and each \ac{V2V}
receiver is placed at a random distance between 10 and \SI{50}{\meter} from its transmitter, in a random
direction. The positions do not change during an episode: at \SI{30}{\meter\per\second}
(\SI{108}{\kilo\meter\per\hour}), a vehicle moves \SI{6}{\meter} in \SI{0.2}{\second}, little compared with
the distances that matter in the cell. Mobility appears instead as a change of geometry from one episode to
the next.

Two pairs are \termdef{neighbors} if their transmitters are at most \SI{300}{\meter} apart, the maximum
communication radius that \citet{li+22fedmarl} take from the \ac{3GPP} \cite{3gpp16v2x}. The set of
neighbors of pair $k$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Nset_k = \bigl\{ k' \in \Kset \setminus \{k\} : \lVert \bm{x}_{k'} - \bm{x}_k \rVert \le \SI{300}{\meter} \bigr\}
  \label{eq:sm-neighbors}
\end{equation}
where $\bm{x}_k$ is the position of the transmitter of pair $k$ and $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ is the Euclidean
distance. Since the positions change in every episode, so do the neighbors of each pair.

\section{Channel model}
% Las ganancias de todos los enlaces (gran y pequeña escala), la distancia mínima y qué ganancias conoce cada agente.
\label{sec:sm-channel}

Every channel gain is the product of a \termdef{large-scale fading} $\alpha$, which depends only on the
distance, and a \termdef{small-scale fading} $g$, which is drawn anew in every slot:
\begin{equation}
  h = \alpha\, g, \qquad \alpha = \varphi\, \max(d, d_{\mathrm{min}})^{-e}, \qquad g \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)
  \label{eq:sm-channel}
\end{equation}
\begin{wherelist}
  \item $d$ is the distance between the transmitter and the receiver, with a floor
    $d_{\mathrm{min}} = \SI{1}{\meter}$ that avoids infinite gains;
  \item $\varphi = \SI{-68.5}{dB}$ and $e = 1.61$ are the constants of the path-loss law;
  \item $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ is an exponential distribution with mean 1. This is Rayleigh fading: the
    amplitude of the signal follows a Rayleigh distribution, and its power an exponential one.
\end{wherelist}
\citet{li+22fedmarl} use three path-loss laws, depending on whether there is a line of sight between the
vehicles \cite[Eq.~4]{li+22fedmarl}. This thesis uses a single one, with the constants above.

Five kinds of link appear in the model:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $h_k^t[m]$: from the transmitter to the receiver of pair $k$, on channel $m$ (its own link);
  \item $h_{m,\mathrm{B}}^t$: from \ac{CUE} $m$ to the \ac{BS};
  \item $h_{k,\mathrm{B}}^t$: from the transmitter of pair $k$ to the \ac{BS};
  \item $h_{m,k}^t$: from \ac{CUE} $m$ to the receiver of pair $k$;
  \item $h_{k',k}^t$: from the transmitter of pair $k'$ to the receiver of pair $k$.
\end{itemize}
The own link has a different small-scale fading on each channel, because each channel occupies a different
band of frequencies. The gains $h_k^t[m]$ and $h_{m,\mathrm{B}}^t$ are drawn at the start of the slot and
are part of what the agent observes. The others are drawn when the outcome of the slot is computed, and no
agent knows them.

\section{Signal quality and rates}
% SINR de cada CUE en la BS y de cada par V2V, y las tasas de Shannon que resultan.
\label{sec:sm-sinr}

The \ac{SINR} of \ac{CUE} $m$ at the \ac{BS} compares its signal with the noise plus the interference of all
the pairs that chose its channel \cite[Eq.~5]{li+22fedmarl}:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_m^{\mathrm{c},t} = \frac{P^{\mathrm{c}}\, h_{m,\mathrm{B}}^t}
    {\sigma^2 + \sum_{k \in \Kset} \rho_k^t[m]\, P_k^t\, h_{k,\mathrm{B}}^t}
  \label{eq:sm-sinr-cue}
\end{equation}
\begin{wherelist}
  \item $\sigma^2 = \SI{-114}{dBm}$ is the noise power in \SI{1}{\mega\hertz};
  \item the sum runs over the pairs that chose channel $m$, since $\rho_k^t[m] = 0$ for the others, and
    each one adds its power times its gain toward the \ac{BS}.
\end{wherelist}

The \ac{SINR} of pair $k$, which transmits on channel $m = m_k^t$, compares the signal of its own
transmitter with the noise, the interference of the \ac{CUE} that owns the channel, and that of the other
pairs on the same channel \cite[Eq.~6]{li+22fedmarl}:
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_k^t = \frac{P_k^t\, h_k^t[m]}
    {\sigma^2 + P^{\mathrm{c}}\, h_{m,k}^t + \sum_{k' \ne k} \rho_{k'}^t[m]\, P_{k'}^t\, h_{k',k}^t}
  \label{eq:sm-sinr-v2v}
\end{equation}
Following \citet{li+22fedmarl}, an \ac{SINR} is written $\gamma$. It always carries a subscript, which
distinguishes it from the discount factor $\gamma$ of Section~\ref{sec:bg-rl}.

The rate of each link follows from its \ac{SINR} with the formula of Shannon
(Equation~\ref{eq:bg-sinr}) \cite[Eq.~8]{li+22fedmarl}:
\begin{equation}
  R_m^{\mathrm{c},t} = W \log_2\bigl(1 + \gamma_m^{\mathrm{c},t}\bigr), \qquad
  R_k^t = W \log_2\bigl(1 + \gamma_k^t\bigr)
  \label{eq:sm-rates}
\end{equation}
where $W = \SI{1}{\mega\hertz}$ is the bandwidth of each channel. A silent pair has a rate of zero.

\section{Queue and delay}
% Dinámica de la cola de cada transmisor con llegada constante de 1 Mbps, el desborde y la relación con el retardo.
\label{sec:sm-queue}

Each \ac{V2V} transmitter receives data to send at a constant rate $\lambda = \SI{1}{Mbps}$, that is,
$\tau\lambda = 1000$ bits per slot. The data it cannot send yet wait in its queue. If $Q_k^t$ is the queue at
the start of slot $t$, the queue at the start of the next slot is \cite[Eq.~16]{li+22fedmarl}
\begin{equation}
  Q_k^{t+1} = \max\bigl(0,\; Q_k^t + \tau \lambda - \tau R_k^t \bigr)
  \label{eq:sm-queue}
\end{equation}
\begin{wherelist}
  \item $\tau\lambda$ are the bits that arrive during the slot, and $\tau R_k^t$ the bits that the link can
    send during the slot;
  \item the maximum with zero keeps the queue from becoming negative: if the link could send more than is
    waiting, the queue is simply emptied.
\end{wherelist}
By Little's law (Section~\ref{sec:bg-v2v}), the delay of the data is $D_k^t = Q_k^t / \lambda$. The maximum
tolerable delay of \ac{V2V} messages is $\Dmax = \SI{100}{\milli\second}$ \cite{3gpp16v2x}, so requiring
$D_k^t \le \Dmax$ is the same as requiring that the queue not exceed
$\Qmax = \lambda \Dmax = \num{100000}$ bits \cite[Sec.~II-C]{li+22fedmarl}.

With these values, a pair that transmits usually empties its queue in the same slot, because its rate easily
exceeds \SI{1}{Mbps}. The queue becomes a problem only when the pair stays silent: without transmitting, it
accumulates 1000 bits per slot and reaches $\Qmax$ after 100 slots. This is the only cost that stops a pair
that, to avoid interfering, would rather never transmit.

\section{Reliability}
% Probabilidad de interrupción (outage) con umbral de 5 dB y p_o = 0.1, y cómo se convierte en un umbral sobre la ganancia del enlace.
\label{sec:sm-reliability}

A \ac{V2V} link is in \termdef{outage} when its \ac{SINR} falls below the minimum needed to decode the
messages, $\gamma_{\mathrm{o}} = \SI{5}{dB}$. Since the fast fading is random, outage cannot be ruled out
completely. What is required is that its probability not exceed $p_{\mathrm{o}} = 0.1$
\cite[Eq.~9]{li+22fedmarl}:
\begin{equation}
  \Pr\bigl(\gamma_k^t < \gamma_{\mathrm{o}}\bigr) \le p_{\mathrm{o}}
  \label{eq:sm-outage}
\end{equation}

\citet{li+22fedmarl} turn this condition into one without probabilities, on the large-scale gains
\cite[Eq.~15]{li+22fedmarl}:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{P_k^t\, \alpha_k}{\sigma^2 + I_k^t} \;\ge\;
  \frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{o}}}{\ln\bigl(1/(1 - p_{\mathrm{o}})\bigr)} \eqqcolon \gth
  \label{eq:sm-reliability}
\end{equation}
\begin{wherelist}
  \item $\alpha_k$ is the large-scale gain of the own link of pair $k$;
  \item $\textstyle I_k^t = P^{\mathrm{c}} h_{m,k}^t + \sum_{k' \ne k} \rho_{k'}^t[m]\, P_{k'}^t\, \alpha_{k',k}$ is
    the interference: that of the \ac{CUE} of the channel plus that of the other pairs on the channel, the
    latter with their large-scale gains $\alpha_{k',k}$;
  \item $\gth$ is the \termdef{reliability threshold}. With $\gamma_{\mathrm{o}} = \SI{5}{dB}$, a factor of
    3.16, and $p_{\mathrm{o}} = 0.1$, it is $\gth = 3.16 / \ln(1/0.9) \approx 30$, about \SI{14.8}{dB}.
\end{wherelist}
The idea behind this step is easiest to see if the interference is held fixed and only the fast fading $g$
of the own link is random. The link then fails if
$g < x \coloneqq \gamma_{\mathrm{o}} (\sigma^2 + I_k^t) / (P_k^t \alpha_k)$, which happens with
probability $1 - e^{-x}$, since $g$ is exponential with mean 1. Requiring $1 - e^{-x} \le p_{\mathrm{o}}$
is the same as requiring $x \le \ln(1/(1-p_{\mathrm{o}}))$, which is Equation~\ref{eq:sm-reliability}.
\citet{li+22fedmarl} also account for the fading of the interfering pairs and, through an upper bound, reach
the same condition. In words:
to withstand fading, the link must operate with a margin. An average \ac{SINR} of \SI{5}{dB} is not
enough; it needs about \SI{14.8}{dB}.

To measure how close a pair is to meeting the condition, we define its \termdef{reliability margin}:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_k^t = \frac{P_k^t\, \alpha_k}{\sigma^2 + I_k^t} - \gth
  \label{eq:sm-margin}
\end{equation}
If $\mu_k^t \ge 0$, the pair meets the condition in that slot; if $\mu_k^t < 0$, it does not. A silent pair
has $\mu_k^t = -\gth$: staying silent counts as failing the reliability condition.

\section{The optimization problem}
% Maximizar la suma de las tasas de los CUE sujeta a tasa mínima, retardo máximo y confiabilidad de los pares, como lo plantean Li et al
\label{sec:sm-problem}

\citet{li+22fedmarl} state the goal of the system as maximizing the rate of the \acp{CUE} while guaranteeing
the quality of service of everyone \cite[Eq.~19]{li+22fedmarl}. In the notation of this chapter:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \max_{\{m_k^t,\, P_k^t\}} \quad & \frac{1}{TM} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \sum_{m \in \Mset} R_m^{\mathrm{c},t} \\
    \text{subject to} \quad & R_m^{\mathrm{c},t} \ge \Rmin, \quad m \in \Mset, \\
      & Q_k^t \le \Qmax, \quad k \in \Kset, \\
      & \mu_k^t \ge 0, \quad k \in \Kset, \\
      & m_k^t \in \Mset,\; P_k^t \in \mathcal{P}, \quad k \in \Kset
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:sm-problem}
\end{equation}
\begin{wherelist}
  \item the objective is the mean rate of the \acp{CUE}, averaged over a horizon of $T$ slots;
  \item the constraints hold in every slot $t$. They ask, in order, for a minimum rate
    $\Rmin = \SI{1}{Mbps}$ for each \ac{CUE}, a bounded delay for each pair, and a reliable link for each
    pair, and they restrict each pair to one channel and one of the power levels.
\end{wherelist}
\citet{li+22fedmarl} also bound the time-averaged queue \cite[Eq.~18]{li+22fedmarl}. The reward that turns
the problem into a learning problem (Section~\ref{sec:sm-shared-reward}) checks only the bound in every
slot, so this thesis leaves the time-averaged one out.

The constraints pull in opposite directions: protecting the \acp{CUE} calls for transmitting little, and
meeting the delay and the reliability of the pairs calls for transmitting. Solving this problem centrally
in every slot would require knowing all the channel gains of the cell, including those that no node
observes. The queues also couple the slots: what a pair decides now changes the queue with which it starts
the next slot. For these reasons, \citet{li+22fedmarl} cast it as a multi-agent reinforcement learning
problem.

\section{Formulation as a multi-agent learning problem}
% Cada par V2V es un agente; juntos forman un Dec-POMDP.
\label{sec:sm-formulation}

Each \ac{V2V} pair is an agent: its transmitter observes, decides, and learns. Together, the agents form a
\ac{Dec-POMDP} (Section~\ref{sec:bg-marl}). None of them observes the full state of the cell, all of them
act at the same time, and, in the original scheme, all of them receive the same reward.

\subsection{Observation}
% El vector de 46 números que observa cada agente: su enlace en cada canal, cada CUE hacia la BS, vecinos en cada canal y su cola.
\label{sec:sm-observation}

In each slot, agent $k$ observes a vector of $3M + 1 = 46$ numbers \cite[Sec.~IV-A]{li+22fedmarl}:
\begin{equation}
  s_k^t = \bigl[\, \tilde{\bm{h}}_k^t,\; \tilde{\bm{h}}_{\mathrm{B}}^t,\; \bm{N}_k^{t-1},\;
    Q_k^t / \Qmax \,\bigr]
  \label{eq:sm-observation}
\end{equation}
\begin{wherelist}
  \item $\tilde{\bm{h}}_k^t = (\tilde h_k^t[1], \dots, \tilde h_k^t[M])$ is the gain of its own link on each
    channel, and $\tilde{\bm{h}}_{\mathrm{B}}^t = (\tilde h_{1,\mathrm{B}}^t, \dots,
    \tilde h_{M,\mathrm{B}}^t)$ the gain of each \ac{CUE} toward the \ac{BS};
  \item $\tilde h = (10 \log_{10} h + 100) / 10$ is a gain in decibels, centered at \SI{-100}{dB} and in
    units of \SI{10}{dB}. This change of scale does not alter the problem; it only keeps the inputs of the
    neural network near 1, which makes training easier;
  \item $\bm{N}_k^{t-1} = (N_k^{t-1}[1], \dots, N_k^{t-1}[M])$, where $N_k^{t-1}[m]$ is the number of
    neighbors of pair $k$ that chose channel $m$ in the previous slot;
  \item $Q_k^t / \Qmax$ is the queue as a fraction of its maximum.
\end{wherelist}
According to \citet{li+22fedmarl}, an agent can obtain all this information with little signaling. The
receiver estimates the gains of the own link and reports them to the transmitter, the \ac{BS} broadcasts
the gains of the \acp{CUE}, the choices of the neighbors are known by sensing the channel or through the
control channel between vehicles, and the queue is local.

\subsection{Action}
% La pareja canal y potencia: 15 canales por 4 niveles, 60 acciones.
\label{sec:sm-action}

The action of agent $k$ is the pair of channel and power, $a_k^t = (m_k^t, P_k^t) \in \Mset \times
\mathcal{P}$. There are $M (N_p + 1) = 15 \times 4 = 60$ possible actions.

\subsection{Shared reward}
% La recompensa compartida que calcula la BS al final de cada slot: términos de tasa de los CUE, de colas y de confiabilidad de los pares, con sus pesos lambda.
\label{sec:sm-shared-reward}

\citet{li+22fedmarl} turn the three constraints of Equation~\ref{eq:sm-problem} into a single reward, which
the \ac{BS} computes at the end of each slot and broadcasts to all the agents. In the version implemented in
this thesis, the reward of slot $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    r^t = {} & \lambda_1 \sum_{m \in \Mset} U\Bigl( \frac{R_m^{\mathrm{c},t} - \Rmin}{\Rmin} \Bigr) \\
      & + \lambda_2 \sum_{k \in \Kset} U\Bigl( \frac{\Qmax - Q_k^t}{\Qmax} \Bigr) \\
      & + \lambda_3 \sum_{k \in \Kset} U\Bigl( \frac{\mu_k^t}{\gth} \Bigr)
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:sm-shared-reward}
\end{equation}
with the utility function \cite[Eq.~23]{li+22fedmarl}
\begin{equation}
  U(x) =
  \begin{cases}
    x, & \text{if } x > 0 , \\
    A, & \text{if } x \le 0
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:sm-utility}
\end{equation}
\begin{wherelist}
  \item the first term rewards each \ac{CUE} for how much its rate exceeds the minimum, and penalizes it if
    the rate falls short;
  \item the second rewards each pair for the free room left in its queue, and penalizes it if the queue
    exceeds the maximum;
  \item the third rewards each pair for its reliability margin, and penalizes it if the pair fails the
    condition of Equation~\ref{eq:sm-reliability};
  \item $U$ returns the margin when the constraint holds and a fixed penalty $A < 0$ when it does not; this
    thesis uses $A = -10$;
  \item $\lambda_1 = 0.3$, $\lambda_2 = 0.5$, and $\lambda_3 = 0.2$ are the weights of the terms
    \cite[Table~III]{li+22fedmarl}.
\end{wherelist}
This is Equation~22 of \citet{li+22fedmarl}, with two changes. The first corrects the sign of the queue term.
The printed equation uses $U(Q_k^t - \Qmax)$, which would reward an overflowing queue, against the constraint
that the term stands for. The second divides each margin by its threshold, so that the three terms have no
units and can be compared with $A$. In their original units (bits per second, bits, and linear \ac{SINR}),
the term of the \acp{CUE} is of the order of $10^7$ per slot, that of the queues of the order of $10^5$, and
that of reliability of the order of $10^2$. The reward would then reflect only the rate of the \acp{CUE},
and the penalty $A$ would have no effect.

The \termdef{reward per episode} is the sum of $r^t$ over the 200 slots of an episode. It is the main measure
of Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, and every method is judged by it, whether or not it learns from it. Its scale
helps to read the results. A \ac{CUE} with \SI{7.7}{Mbps} adds $0.3 \times 6.7 \approx 2$ per slot, so the
15 \acp{CUE} add about 30 per slot, about 6000 per episode. By contrast, each pair that fails the
reliability condition subtracts $0.2 \times 10 = 2$ per slot, and each overflowing queue subtracts
$0.5 \times 10 = 5$. With many pairs, the penalties outweigh the rewards, and the reward per episode becomes
negative.

\paragraph{An example.} Consider a tiny network with one channel, its \ac{CUE}, and two pairs, A and B, that
chose that channel. In one slot, the \ac{CUE} achieves \SI{5}{Mbps}, pair A transmits, and pair B stays
silent:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item term of the \ac{CUE}: $0.3 \times U\bigl((5-1)/1\bigr) = 0.3 \times 4 = 1.2$;
  \item pair A, with \num{20000} bits in its queue and a large-scale \ac{SINR} of 60 (margin
    $60 - 30 = 30$): queue $0.5 \times U(0.8) = 0.4$ and reliability $0.2 \times U(30/30) = 0.2$;
  \item pair B, with \num{100500} bits in its queue, more than the maximum, and silent: queue
    $0.5 \times A = -5$ and reliability $0.2 \times A = -2$.
\end{itemize}
The shared reward of the slot is $1.2 + 0.4 + 0.2 - 5 - 2 = -5.2$, and both pairs receive it. Pair A, which
acted well, receives the same bad news as pair B. Section~\ref{sec:pa-individual-reward} returns to this
example to show what changes with the individual reward.

\subsection{Objective of each agent}
% Cada agente maximiza su retorno descontado; con la recompensa compartida debe descubrir qué parte del resultado depende de su propia acción.
\label{sec:sm-objective}

Each agent seeks the policy that maximizes its expected return (Equation~\ref{eq:bg-return}), with discount
factor $\gamma$. With the shared reward, every agent receives the same $r^t$, and each one must discover,
from that number alone, which part of the outcome depends on its own action.

\subsection{What happens in a slot}
% El orden de los eventos en un slot: observar, elegir, calcular SINR, tasas y colas, recibir la recompensa y aprender.
\label{sec:sm-slot}

To close, this is the order of events in each slot $t$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item Each agent observes $s_k^t$ (Equation~\ref{eq:sm-observation}).
  \item Each agent chooses its channel and its power, without knowing what the others choose.
  \item The environment draws the fading of the links that no one observes and computes the \acp{SINR}
    (Equations~\ref{eq:sm-sinr-cue} and~\ref{eq:sm-sinr-v2v}), the rates (Equation~\ref{eq:sm-rates}), the
    margins (Equation~\ref{eq:sm-margin}), and the queues of the next slot (Equation~\ref{eq:sm-queue}).
  \item The environment computes the reward from which each agent learns.
  \item The observable gains of the next slot are drawn, and each agent receives its new observation.
\end{enumerate}
