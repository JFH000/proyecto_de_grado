# Summary: "Federated Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning for Resource Allocation of Vehicle-to-Vehicle Communications"

**Link:** [ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9770401](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9770401/) — *IEEE Transactions on Vehicular Technology*, Vol. 71, No. 8, Aug 2022 — not on arXiv
**Authors:** Xiang Li, Lingyun Lu, Wei Ni, Abbas Jamalipour, Dalin Zhang, Haifeng Du (Beijing Jiaotong Univ. / CSIRO Australia / Univ. of Sydney)
**Type:** Research article, simulation-validated

## What the paper covers

**System.** A single-cell C-V2X network: `M` cellular users (CUEs) hold `M` pre-assigned orthogonal channels; `K` V2V pairs must decide, in a decentralized manner, which CUE channel to reuse and what transmit power to use, without disrupting the CUEs or colliding with other V2V pairs. Each V2V pair *is* an agent — there is no separate "controller" agent.

**MDP formulation (Sec. IV-A, the core detail for this project):**
- **State per agent (3M+1 dimensional):** (1) its own V2V link's channel coefficient `h_k^t[m]` for every channel `m`; (2) the channel coefficients of all CUEs `h_{m,B}^t[m]`; (3) the *previous-slot* channel selections of neighboring V2V pairs within a 300 m radius; (4) its own transmitter queue length `Q_k^t`. Only local/locally-observable/one-slot-delayed neighbor information — no agent ever sees another agent's private state or the true global state directly.
- **Action:** joint choice of channel `ζ_{k,m}` and a discretized transmit power level (`N_p+1` levels) — action space size `M·(N_p+1)`.
- **Reward:** a **single shared/team reward** `R^t` (Eq. 22) combining a CUE-rate-requirement penalty, a V2V queue/delay penalty, and a V2V reliability/interference term. Critically, this reward is **computed centrally at the base station from joint network state** and then **broadcast identically to every agent** — so the reward signal itself needs global information even though each agent's *observation* and *policy* stay local.

**Algorithm (Sec. IV-B/C):** each agent runs its own **Dueling Double DQN (D3QN)** with prioritized experience replay, trained purely on its own local (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples — this is DTE (Decentralized Training and Execution) in Amato's formalism (`summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md`): no agent's training loop ever touches another agent's history/observations. **Federated Learning (FedAvg) is layered on top**: every few slots, each agent uploads its D3QN weights to the BS, the BS computes a weighted average `θ^(g) = Σ_k p_k θ_k^(g)` (`p_k` = agent k's minibatch-size share), and broadcasts the averaged weights back — classic **centralized client-server FL**, not gossip/decentralized (contrast with `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md`'s DFL). Stated FedAvg complexity: `O((BK+K)·log(1/ς))`, linear in agent count `K`.

**Why they added FL, in the paper's own words:** *"Since the states observed by each V2V link cannot fully characterize the entire vehicular environment, FL provides an effective method for improving the model performance with decentralized local DRL models"* — i.e., FL's explicit purpose here is to give each locally-trained, partially-observing agent implicit access to network-wide information it never directly observed.

**Experiments (Sec. V, Tables II/III):** 15 CUEs/channels fixed; **V2V pair count swept over {5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30}** — this is the paper's own agent-count scaling variable. Baselines: Greedy (GM, always max power/lowest-interference channel), Clustering (CM, 5 fixed geographic clusters, deferred-acceptance matching within each), and Single-Agent DRL (SADRL, only one V2V pair actually learns; others just replicate its model — i.e., FL with K=1 real learner). Replay buffer 10,000, minibatch 128, Adam, reward weights `(λ1,λ2,λ3)=(0.3,0.5,0.2)`, target-network update every 400 slots.

**Key scaling result (Figs. 3, 4, 8, 10):** CUE average rate **declines monotonically as V2V-pair count grows** for every method (≈8 Mb at K=5 → ≈4.8 Mb at K=30 for the proposed method) — more agents means more channel reuse contention and interference, the opposite direction from Xu et al.'s finding. But the *degradation slope* is flattest for the proposed FedMARL method (it stays clearly above Clustering and Greedy at every K). Transmission delay (Fig. 8) grows with both packet size and V2V-pair count for all methods, but only the proposed method stays under the 100 ms delay requirement even at K=30; Greedy and Clustering both breach it. Fig. 10 additionally shows that reward convergence itself takes more training episodes as agent count grows — a training-time scaling cost distinct from the performance-degradation one.

## Connection to the project

**This paper is a working, published instance of exactly the pattern flagged as an open question in `summary_frl_techniques_survey.md` and `summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md`.** Those two summaries independently concluded that CTDE's centralized-critic mechanism doesn't transfer cleanly to a federated regime, and speculated that "independent DTE learners + periodic federated parameter aggregation" might be the practical federated substitute for what a centralized critic would otherwise provide. **Li et al. is a direct, explicit confirmation of that substitution being real, not hypothetical**: the quoted justification above is literally "our agents can't see the whole network locally, so we use FedAvg to give them that missing information" — FL is being used here as a stand-in for centralized-training information-sharing, applied to agents whose training and execution are otherwise pure DTE.

**This doesn't fit either of Qi et al.'s HFRL/VFRL categories cleanly (a genuinely new data point for Node 5's taxonomy work).** HFRL (per `summary_frl_techniques_survey.md`) assumes each agent has its *own independent copy* of the environment; VFRL assumes agents split *feature/state space* of one shared environment via encrypted fusion. Here, all `K` V2V agents act **simultaneously in one single shared environment** (their joint actions jointly determine interference and the one shared reward) — not independent environment copies — yet aggregation is plain unencrypted FedAvg over model weights, not VFRL's encrypted intermediate-feature fusion. This is a **third pattern**: one shared environment, DTE-style local training/execution, shared *reward* computed centrally, and FedAvg used purely for periodic knowledge-sharing between agents rather than for privacy. Worth naming explicitly as a case the Qi et al. taxonomy doesn't cover, when drafting the answer for Nicolás.

**For Node 3's scenario table:** # agents = 5 to 30 V2V pairs (only scenario in the set with an explicit, wide, controlled sweep over agent count on the same architecture); observability = strictly local + one-slot-delayed neighbor info (300 m radius), no raw joint observation ever centralized; reward = single shared/team reward, computed centrally but fed back as a scalar only; paradigm = **DTE + centralized-server FedAvg**, no CTDE critic anywhere.

**For Node 6's experiment-scaling table:** paper | scenario | # agents | what they measured → *Li et al. (2022) | V2V/C-V2X, 15 fixed CUEs | 5/10/15/20/25/30 V2V pairs | CUE avg. rate (Mb), V2V transmission delay (ms) vs. training episode, both vs. agent count*. Contrast explicitly with Xu et al. (`summary_frl_secure_incentive_icps.md`), where more agents *helped* convergence — here more agents strictly hurts raw performance (more interference/contention) even though the proposed method degrades more gracefully than baselines; the mechanism driving scaling behavior is domain-specific (shared spectrum contention vs. Xu et al.'s independent per-block federated experience), not a general property of "more agents in FRL."

**Open gap worth flagging for Node 7:** the paper's own future-work explicitly leaves the BS as a permanent central aggregator and only proposes *adaptive switching* between decentralized and BS-coordinated modes — it never asks whether the FedAvg step itself could be decentralized (gossip/consensus, per `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md`) while keeping this same "DTE + periodic weight-sharing as CTDE substitute" pattern. Combining Li et al.'s core mechanism (federated weight-sharing standing in for a centralized critic) with a serverless aggregation topology is a concrete, currently-unaddressed combination — and unlike Xu et al.'s case, here the underlying task (shared spectrum, shared reward) plausibly requires exactly the kind of neighbor-only gossip topology `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md` describes, since V2V pairs are already defined by local (300 m) neighborhoods.
