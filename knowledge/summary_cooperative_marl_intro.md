# Summary: "An Initial Introduction to Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning"

**arXiv:** [2405.06161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06161)
**Author:** Christopher Amato, Northeastern University
**Type:** Tutorial/monograph (book-length introduction, ~90 pages), not a comprehensive survey — the author explicitly curates only the methods he considers essential for understanding the area, and recommends Sutton & Barto (single-agent RL) and the Albrecht/Christianos/Schäfer MARL book as complements.

## What the paper covers

The text organizes all of cooperative MARL (agents share one joint reward) around a three-way taxonomy of *training/execution regimes*, crossed with a *value-based vs. policy-gradient* algorithmic split. Structure:

1. **The Dec-POMDP formalism** (Ch. 1) — the paper's foundation. A Dec-POMDP is the tuple ⟨I, S, {Aᵢ}, T, R, {Oᵢ}, O, H, γ⟩: a finite/continuous set of agents, a *true* underlying state (explicitly reserved for this — many papers misuse "state" for joint observations/histories), per-agent action and observation sets, shared transition and reward functions, and a single joint reward R(s, **a**) (this shared reward is what makes it "cooperative" as opposed to a general POSG). Each agent's policy πᵢ is a mapping from *local action-observation histories* hᵢ (not just the last observation — reactive/Markov policies are flagged as a strictly weaker special case) to actions. Complexity result cited: solving an MDP is P-complete, a POMDP is PSPACE-complete, a Dec-POMDP is NEXP-complete.

2. **Three training/execution paradigms** (the paper's central taxonomy):
   - **CTE** (Centralized Training and Execution) — a single centralized controller acts on the full joint history at both training *and* execution; reduces to single-agent RL/planning over joint action/observation spaces (formalized here as the **MMDP**, fully observable, and **MPOMDP**, partially observable, models). Scales poorly (exponential joint spaces) but is the upper bound on achievable performance since it permits arbitrary coordination.
   - **DTE** (Decentralized Training and Execution) — the fewest assumptions: each agent only ever sees its own history, action, and observation, both at training and at execution; the shared reward is the only cross-agent signal the learning algorithm gets. Required whenever there is no centralized simulator/offline phase available.
   - **CTDE** (Centralized Training for Decentralized Execution) — the most popular paradigm; centralized information is used only during an offline/simulated training phase, and execution is purely decentralized (per-agent histories only). The paper stresses that CTDE "has never been formally defined" in the literature and adopts a deliberately broad reading: *any* centralized information/coordination during learning that would not be available at decentralized execution counts as CTDE.

3. **Value-based methods across the three regimes** — Q-learning/DQN/DRQN background, then DTE value methods: Independent Q-Learning (IQL, treats other agents as environment, provably nonstationary/non-convergent in general), and fixes that trade off optimism vs. noise-robustness: Distributed Q-learning (optimistic max-based update), Hysteretic Q-learning (two learning rates, α>β, for positive vs. negative TD-errors), Lenient Q-learning (per-history-action temperature-based leniency schedule). Deep extensions: Independent DRQN, Dec-HDRQN with a "concurrent experience replay trajectory" (CERT) buffer to fix the decorrelation-across-agents problem that naive independent replay buffers cause.

4. **Policy-gradient methods across regimes** — Decentralized REINFORCE (Peshkin & Savova 2000) is highlighted for a specific theoretical result: the joint policy gradient decomposes exactly into the sum of independent per-agent gradients under concurrent (synchronized) learning, giving DTE policy-gradient methods actual local-optimum convergence guarantees that DTE value-based methods lack. Independent Actor-Critic (IAC) and Independent PPO (IPPO) follow the same pattern.

5. **CTDE algorithms — two families, the heart of the paper**:
   - **Value function factorization** — VDN (additive decomposition Qtot ≈ Σᵢ Qᵢ(hᵢ,aᵢ)), QMIX (generalizes to a monotonic mixing function via non-negative-weight hypernetworks, formalized by the **Individual-Global-Max (IGM)** property: argmax over the joint Q equals the tuple of per-agent argmaxes), and pointers to QTRAN and QPLEX as further relaxations of the monotonicity constraint.
   - **Centralized-critic actor-critic** — a shared/centralized critic Q̂(**h**, **a**) (or V̂(**h**)) trained on joint histories/actions/reward is used only to compute each decentralized actor's policy gradient; the actor πᵢ(aᵢ|hᵢ) itself never sees anything beyond its own history. Covers MADDPG (continuous/deterministic actions, off-policy, replay buffer), COMA (counterfactual baseline for credit assignment: Aᵢ(**h**,**a**) = Q(**h**,**a**) − Σ_{aᵢ'} πᵢ(aᵢ'|hᵢ)Q(**h**,aᵢ',**a**₋ᵢ)), and MAPPO/IPPO (PPO-style clipped objective; IPPO uses a per-agent local critic but still counts as CTDE once parameter sharing is used).

6. **A notable theoretical/critical thread**: the paper argues **state-based critics** (using the true state s instead of joint history **h**) are *provably unsound/biased* in genuinely partially-observable Dec-POMDPs (illustrated with Dec-Tiger), despite being used by MADDPG, COMA, and vanilla MAPPO — they lose the history-dependent information the decentralized actor relies on. **History-state critics** Q̂(**h**, s, **a**) are shown to be the unbiased, best-performing choice. The paper also shows CTDE and DTE policy-gradient methods are theoretically near-equivalent under the concurrent-learning assumption — CTDE's centralization does not guarantee better performance and can hurt scalability/variance.

## Connection to the thesis (precise CTDE definition for comparison against Qi et al. 2021's Federated RL taxonomy)

For the taxonomy-comparison task (matching this against Horizontal/Vertical Federated RL from Qi et al. 2021), the operative, technical content of CTDE as formalized in this paper is:

**CTDE, precisely, assumes an asymmetric two-phase information regime with a discontinuity between the phases:**

- **Training phase (centralized):** a single learning algorithm — separate from the agents themselves — has *simultaneous, synchronous, unconstrained access* to the full joint trajectory at every step: the joint action-observation histories **h** = ⟨h₁,...,h_n⟩, the joint action **a**, the joint observation **o**, the single (shared, since cooperative) reward r, and optionally the ground-truth environment state s. This access is assumed to come from a centralized simulator or an offline/logging setup, and is modeled as free and instantaneous (no bandwidth limits, no privacy constraints, no communication cost or delay). The centralized apparatus can take two architectural forms: (a) a **centralized critic** Q̂(**h**,**a**) or V̂(**h**) (MADDPG/COMA/MAPPO), whose gradient with respect to Q̂ is used to update each agent's actor parameters ψᵢ; or (b) a **factored joint value function** Qtot(**h**,**a**) ≈ f(Q₁(h₁,a₁),...,Q_n(h_n,a_n)) (VDN/QMIX), trained end-to-end via a joint TD loss, with f constrained (e.g., monotonicity, satisfying IGM) so that decentralized per-agent argmax reproduces the centralized joint argmax.
- **Execution phase (decentralized):** the centralized critic or mixing network is *discarded/inactive*. Each agent retains only its own decentralized policy πᵢ(aᵢ|hᵢ) or utility Qᵢ(hᵢ,aᵢ), a function *exclusively* of that agent's own local action-observation history — never of **h**, **a**, **o**, or s, and with no runtime communication between agents.
- The paper is explicit that this is a *broad, informally-adopted* umbrella term ("CTDE has never been formally defined... I will take a wide view... any centralized information or coordination during learning that is not available during decentralized execution") — not a rigorously partitioned data/communication model.

The key structural feature for contrast with Federated RL: CTDE's "centralization" is a **temporal/informational discontinuity within a single training pipeline** (one algorithm sees everything during an offline/simulated phase, then the resulting decentralized artifacts are deployed with no further access), assumed to have **zero communication cost or privacy constraint** during that phase. This is a fundamentally different axis from Horizontal/Vertical FRL, which partition *persistent, physically-separate data holders* (clients/agents) along either the sample axis (horizontal: same feature/action space, disjoint experience) or the feature axis (vertical: same samples/entities, disjoint observation/state features), and which explicitly model **constrained, costly, round-based communication** (periodic parameter/gradient aggregation, e.g., FedAvg-style) and a **no-raw-data-sharing** privacy constraint between clients that persists at both training and deployment. In other words: CTDE assumes the centralization constraint disappears after training and imposes no cost while it exists; Federated RL assumes decentralization is a standing constraint (privacy/bandwidth) that must be respected *during* training via bounded, periodic communication of model updates rather than raw joint trajectories. This distinction should be the crux of the CTDE-vs-HFRL/VFRL comparison: CTDE's centralized critic is not a "federated aggregator" in disguise — it presupposes exactly the free, instantaneous joint-data channel that federated learning is designed to avoid needing.

## Suggested next reads
- The `marl-book` (Albrecht, Christianos, Schäfer) for a broader, more textbook-style MARL treatment.
- QTRAN and QPLEX papers directly, for the precise relaxations of QMIX's monotonicity/IGM constraint (only summarized secondhand here).
- The JAIR23/AAAI22 papers Amato repeatedly cites on state-based vs. history-state critic bias — relevant if the thesis needs to argue about what "centralized information" is safe vs. unsound to use in a federated critic design.
