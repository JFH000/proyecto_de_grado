# Summary: "A Comprehensive Survey on Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Connected and Automated Vehicles"

**Link:** [DOI 10.3390/s23104710](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23104710) — *Sensors* 23(10):4710, MDPI, 2023 (open access, CC BY). Also indexed as [PMC10221654](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10221654/). Not on arXiv. Retrieved via the Europe PMC full-text-XML mirror (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10221654/fullTextXML`) — both the PMC and MDPI direct PDF links are gated behind a JS proof-of-work/bot check that plain HTTP tools can't solve; Europe PMC's mirror isn't gated and returns the complete JATS XML instead.
**Authors:** Pamul Yadav, Ashutosh Mishra, Shiho Kim (Yonsei University)
**Type:** Survey, paper-catalog style (organizes ~40 individual works by application task rather than by algorithm family)

## What the paper covers

**Section 2 — MARL formalism and three learning paradigms**, terminology that needs explicit reconciliation with Amato's CTE/DTE/CTDE (`summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md`) since this survey uses different names for the same three regimes:
- **DTDE** (Decentralized Training and Decentralized Execution) = Amato's **DTE**. Each agent's policy maps only its local observations to actions; no information sharing during training. Flagged as underperforming centralized paradigms in general, but stabilized in practice via CERT-style decentralized experience replay (Omidshafiei et al. — the same CERT mechanism `summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md` already cites for Dec-HDRQN).
- **CTCE** (Centralized Training and Centralized Execution) = Amato's **CTE**. A single centralized learner produces one joint policy over the full joint action/observation space; requires "unhindered and instant communication." Explicitly named as suffering the curse of dimensionality as agent count grows, and as producing the **"lazy agent problem"** — when reward is fully shared, one agent may have insufficient individual incentive to learn a good policy since its low effort can be masked by teammates, a specific mechanism this paper names that neither `summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md` nor `summary_marl_ad_survey.md` had spelled out this precisely. States CTDE "indigenously" avoids the lazy-agent problem — a mechanistic reason (not just "it scales better") for CTDE's dominance in the literature.
- **CTDE** — same name, same meaning as Amato's: local execution policy πᵢ, centralized-only training-time information sharing.

**Section 3 — MARL for CAVs, organized by task**, the paper's main content, four application areas plus cross-cutting sim-to-real, safety, and benchmarking sections:
- **3.1 Simulators** — ten platforms surveyed: CityFlow, SUMO, Flow (built on SUMO), CARLA, Highway-env, Multi-Car Racing Gym, INTERACTION Visualization Tool, OpenAI Traffic Simulator, Gym-Gazebo, PTV VISSIM, PRESCAN. Overlaps heavily with `summary_marl_ad_survey.md`'s simulator taxonomy (both cover SUMO/Flow/CityFlow/CARLA/MetaDrive-adjacent tools) but this list is broader on the "traffic-signal/scheduling" side (VISSIM, PRESCAN, Gym-Gazebo) vs. the other survey's fidelity/traffic-flow split.
- **3.2.1 Cooperative motion planning** — platooning/CACC, lane changing, merging. Concrete agent-count and mechanism details worth logging: Peake et al. use `N`-simultaneous LSTM blocks chained into a unified CACC controller, tested on platoons of **3 to 5 cars**; Li et al. use curriculum learning plus a dual-channel protocol (state-transmission + reward-transmission) specifically to solve the lazy-agent problem via explicit per-agent reward assignment; Han et al. use **Shapley values** to reallocate a shared team reward among agents by individual contribution (a credit-assignment mechanism, tested in CARLA, shown to beat MADDPG/M3DDPG/COMA) — a different, game-theoretically-grounded alternative to COMA's counterfactual baseline already logged in `summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md`.
- **3.2.2 Trajectory prediction** — dominated by GNN-based methods (Ma et al., Jia et al., Mo et al.), continual-learning approaches to fight catastrophic forgetting as agent count grows, and one anomaly-detection application (Wiederer et al.'s STGAE).
- **3.2.3 Intelligent Traffic Management** — automated intersection management and traffic-signal control. Concrete agent-count data points for a node-6-style table: Prabuchandran et al. test **9-junction and 20-junction** road networks modeled on real Bangalore road networks (VISSIM simulator); Chu et al.'s MA2C introduces a **spatial discount factor** that weakens information transmission from farther agents in the reward function — a concrete, citable mechanism for how a scenario can be "local-communication-only" without being literally DTDE; Cui et al. explicitly test **"hundreds of vehicles"** in an open-network congestion scenario and demonstrate a **fully distributed, communication-free** multi-agent driving policy can still reduce congestion — a second data point (after Li et al.'s V2V paper) of decentralization succeeding at real scale.
- **3.3 Sim-to-real, 3.4 Safety, 3.5 Benchmarking platforms, 3.6 Datasets** — safety section names two concrete mechanisms (uncertainty quantification: epistemic vs. aleatoric; Lyapunov-function-constrained "safe exploration"), datasets are INTERACTION, TrajNet++, NGSIM.

**Section 4 — Discussion/open challenges:** spatiotemporal multi-modal data fusion (GCN/GAT-based), domain adaptation via meta-RL, safety+interpretability, benchmarking-platform gaps, and — explicitly named as "an open challenge for researchers" — **communication/information-transmission delay** in CAV networks. Notably, **this survey never once mentions federated learning, FedAvg, or any privacy-preserving/decentralized-aggregation mechanism** — its notion of "decentralized" (DTDE, or Cui et al.'s communication-free policy) is about training/execution locality, not about a federated aggregation step across a distributed system.

## Connection to the project (reunión-3 checklist, cross-checked against `summary_marl_ad_survey.md`)

**Terminology cross-validation for Node 5:** this is now the *third* independent source (after Amato and the MARL-AD survey) using functionally the same CTE/DTE/CTDE split, just under the labels DTDE/CTCE/CTDE — worth citing as confirmation that the taxonomy is stable across the literature, not an idiosyncrasy of any one paper, when presenting the answer to Nicolás. The **lazy-agent-problem** mechanism this paper names explicitly is a concrete, previously-missing piece of *why* CTE/CTCE is avoided beyond "it doesn't scale" — worth folding into the CTE/DTE/CTDE section of the transferability answer already drafted across `summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md` and `summary_frl_techniques_survey.md`.

**Largely reinforces, rather than extends, `summary_marl_ad_survey.md` on methods/algorithms** — no genuinely new algorithmic paradigm here beyond what Zhang et al.'s survey already covers (CTDE/DTDE split, credit-assignment as the central CTCE pain point, GNN-based interaction modeling). The value-add is a **much larger, more concrete catalog of individual task-specific papers with agent counts and mechanisms**, useful directly for Node 3's scenario table and Node 6's scaling table (Prabuchandran et al.'s 9-vs-20-junction sweep, Cui et al.'s hundreds-of-vehicles congestion result, Peake et al.'s 3-to-5-car platoon, Chu et al.'s spatial-discount-factor communication design) — none of which appeared in the other AD survey's Table 2 SOTA-methods comparison.

**Genuinely new and citable: the total absence of federated learning from this survey.** Given this paper explicitly reviews ~40 CAV-MARL works published up to early 2023 and never once raises FL, privacy-preserving aggregation, or decentralized parameter sharing as a technique *or* as an open challenge, this is direct evidence that **federating MARL for connected/automated vehicles specifically is an unaddressed gap in the AV literature itself**, not merely something this project's prior reading (`summary_fedmarl_v2v_resource_allocation.md`, one paper) happened not to find more of. That single Li et al. FedMARL paper looks, from this survey's vantage point, like an outlier rather than an established sub-line of CAV research — strengthening (not just repeating) the Node 7 case for a federated-CAV-MARL problem candidate.

**For Node 3's scenario table**, this survey adds several finer-grained CAV sub-scenarios beyond the single "V2V/V2X" row already logged from Li et al. and the MARL-AD survey: platooning (3–5 agents, LSTM-chained controllers), unsignalized intersection scheduling (Xu et al.'s FCTP + MADRLS), traffic-signal control at municipal scale (9–20 intersections), and open-network congestion control (hundreds of vehicles, communication-free). Each has a different reward structure (individual Shapley-reallocated reward vs. shared team reward vs. spatially-discounted reward) worth its own row rather than being folded into one generic "cars" entry.

**Nothing here changes or contradicts the draft transferability answer** (CTE/CTCE doesn't transfer to federated regimes cleanly, DTDE/DTE is nearly federation-native, CTDE transfers conditionally) — it reinforces it from a fourth independent angle and supplies the lazy-agent mechanism as an added justification for why CTDE dominates the applied CAV literature specifically.

## Suggested next step
Han et al.'s Shapley-value reward reallocation (ref. [57], tested in CARLA) is a concrete alternative to COMA's counterfactual baseline for credit assignment — worth a closer read if the thesis's eventual method needs a credit-assignment mechanism compatible with a federated (no-central-critic) setting, since Shapley-value computation is more naturally decomposable per-agent than a counterfactual baseline that needs the centralized joint Q-function.
