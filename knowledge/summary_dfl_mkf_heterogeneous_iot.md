# Summary: "A decentralised federated learning scheme for heterogeneous devices in cognitive IoT"

**Link:** [doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcce.2024.08.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcce.2024.08.001) — *International Journal of Cognitive Computing in Engineering* 5 (2024) 357–366, open access (CC BY-NC-ND). Not on arXiv.
**Authors:** Huanhuan Ge, Xingtao Yang, Jinlong Wang, Zhihan Lyu (Qingdao University of Technology / Anhui Jianzhu University / Uppsala University)
**Type:** Research article, proposes and empirically evaluates a new algorithm, **DFL–MKF** (Decentralised FL via Multimodel Knowledge transfer and Fusion)

## What the paper covers

**Problem framing:** classical FL for cognitive IoT (Fig. 1a) relies on a central server, which is a single point of failure, a communication bottleneck, and struggles when edge devices are both data-heterogeneous (non-IID) *and* system-heterogeneous (different compute/storage capability, so they can't all run the same model architecture). The paper's proposed fix removes the server (Fig. 1b, "Decentralised FL") and additionally lets each device run a *different* model architecture ("multimodel").

**DFL–MKF algorithm (Section 3, Algorithm 1):**
- `K` devices, no central server. Each communication round `t`, each device `k` **randomly selects `S` neighbours from the full pool of `K` devices** (not a fixed graph — resampled every round).
- For each selected neighbour `i`, device `k` obtains a **logit** `p^i = model(D_k, θ^i)` — i.e., neighbour `i`'s current model is applied to device `k`'s *own* local data `D_k` to produce class-probability logits (this requires neighbour model parameters `θ^i` to be shared/broadcast — see line 12 of Algorithm 1 — but only the resulting logits, not the parameters themselves, are used in the aggregation step that follows).
- **Knowledge fusion (Eq. 4):** the `S` neighbours' logits are combined into one "teacher" logit via a **weighted average by each neighbour's local dataset size**: `p(θ^T) = Σ_i (N_i / Σ_{k∈S_k} N_k) · p^i`. This is the paper's aggregation mechanism — deliberately not FedAvg-style parameter averaging.
- **Local update (Eq. 5):** device `k`'s model is updated by minimizing task loss (cross-entropy) *plus* a KL-divergence term pulling its own logit toward the fused teacher logit: `min_θ f(θ^k) = Σ L(θ^k, x, y) + α·KL(p(θ^k), p(θ^T))`, with `α` a hyperparameter (0.5 default; paper sweeps 0.1–0.7 and finds a peak at 0.5 — too high makes the local model overly dependent on neighbours, too low forfeits the benefit).
- **System-heterogeneity handling:** devices are assigned *different model architectures/sizes* based on a weighted sum of their computational (`m`) and storage (`n`) capability — e.g. for EMNIST some devices get an MLP, others one of two different CNNs; for CIFAR10/100, CNN vs. modified ResNet18 vs. VGG11. This is only possible because aggregation happens in **logit/output space** (fixed dimensionality = number of classes `C`), not parameter space — architecturally mismatched models can't be averaged, but they can all produce comparably-shaped logit vectors.

**Experiments (Section 4):** EMNIST, CIFAR10, CIFAR100; `K=100` devices, `T=100` communication rounds, `N=10` local epochs, Dirichlet(β) non-IID partitioning (default β=0.3). Baselines: **FedAvg** (centralized), **Ditto** (personalized FL with a regularization term), **DPSGD** (Lian et al. 2017 — a genuine decentralized-SGD baseline, i.e. the closest prior-art comparison for "does decentralization plus heterogeneity actually work"). Results: DFL–MKF beats all three on final accuracy (+0.92%/+2.72%/+2.06% over FedAvg on EMNIST/CIFAR10/CIFAR100 respectively) and on communication efficiency (42%/15%/16% fewer rounds than FedAvg to hit a fixed target accuracy at β=0.3). Neighbour-count sweep (`S`∈{1,5,10,20}): accuracy improves with more neighbours but **plateaus past S=5**, while communication cost keeps growing linearly with S — the paper picks S=5 as the accuracy/cost sweet spot. Larger β (more IID) narrows the gap between DFL–MKF and baselines; the method's edge is largest precisely in the high-non-IID regime.

## Connection to the project (cross-checked against `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md`)

This re-read (per the reunión-3 checklist, node 4) was meant to pin down exactly what topology and aggregation DFL–MKF uses, now that the Martínez Beltrán et al. survey's vocabulary is available. Two findings sharpen — and one **corrects** — what's already logged in `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md`:

1. **Topology: closest to the survey's "random" category, but with a twist the survey doesn't name.** The Beltrán survey's random/Erdős–Rényi topology is described as a graph fixed by proximity/capacity heuristics. DFL–MKF instead **redraws each device's neighbour set independently, uniformly at random, every single communication round** — there is no persistent graph structure at all, just per-round i.i.d. sampling from the full device pool. This is a meaningfully different (and arguably simpler/more robust) design point than any of the four topologies in that survey's taxonomy — worth naming explicitly as "dynamic random resampling" if the thesis's own topology taxonomy needs a fifth category, since it sidesteps topology-design questions (ring diameter, cluster boundaries) entirely at the cost of no persistent trust/locality structure.

2. **Aggregation mechanism: not what `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md` assumed was the only option — this is a real, working *third* alternative.** That earlier summary's "Connection to your thesis" section proposed replacing a CTDE central critic with **DSGD/DFedAvgM/DACFL-style parameter-space consensus** among local critics. DFL–MKF demonstrates a structurally different mechanism — **output-space (logit) knowledge-distillation fusion**, weighted by data quantity — that a parameter-averaging consensus algorithm categorically cannot do: **it works across heterogeneous model architectures.** This directly patches a gap in the earlier proposal: a "federated CTDE" built on DSGD/DACFL-style critic-parameter consensus silently assumes every agent's critic has the same architecture (needed to average parameters element-wise), which is unrealistic for a heterogeneous multi-agent fleet (e.g., drones with different sensor loadouts, vehicles with different onboard compute). DFL–MKF's recipe — apply each neighbour's model to your own local experience, fuse the resulting *value estimates or Q-logits* (not weights) via a distillation loss — is architecture-agnostic and is therefore the more realistic mechanism for decentralizing a critic across heterogeneous agents. This is a concrete, citable answer to the open question left in `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md`.

3. **Confirms the earlier summary's core claim, from cognitive-IoT rather than MARL.** Independent of the correction above, DFL–MKF is a second, working confirmation that removing the central server and going fully peer-to-peer (Beltrán's "DFL" schema, no rotating or fixed aggregator) is not just theoretically appealing but **empirically outperforms centralized FedAvg** under realistic heterogeneity — reinforcing the case (already made from the topology-survey side) that a decentralized design isn't a compromise made purely for fault-tolerance; it can be the better-performing choice.

**For Node 7 (candidate problems):** "logit/output-space knowledge-distillation fusion instead of parameter averaging, for federating heterogeneous-architecture agents in a decentralized MARL setting" is now a concrete, previously-undocumented candidate mechanism worth stating explicitly as a problem-formulation ingredient — distinct from (and complementary to) the gossip/consensus-on-parameters idea and the VDN/QMIX value-factorization idea already logged in `summary_cooperative_marl_intro.md` and `summary_decentralized_fl_survey.md`.

## Suggested next step
DFL–MKF's Eq. 4 (data-quantity-weighted logit fusion) and Eq. 5 (task loss + KL-to-teacher regularizer) are short and directly adaptable — worth sketching, as a thesis proof-of-concept exercise, what "logit" would mean for an RL agent (Q-values over a shared/aligned action space? action-probability distributions from a policy head?) and whether the same weighted-KL-fusion trick would give a workable decentralized, architecture-agnostic critic/policy consensus mechanism for a small drone-swarm or multi-vehicle testbed.
